Collective Dynamics of Colliding Inertial Particles in Prescribed Carrier Flows

How do local particle collisions change the collective organisation generated by inertia and the surrounding flow?

THE RESEARCH QUESTION

From local contact to collective transport

Inertial particles do not follow fluid pathlines exactly. Their finite response time can produce non-uniform particle distributions even in an incompressible carrier flow. Finite particle size adds another ingredient: a contact impulse changes the relative velocity of two particles, influencing their subsequent trajectories and future encounters.

My thesis investigates these mechanisms through three connected stages: numerical hard-particle modelling, controlled alternating sinusoidal shear, and a reduced near-wall turbulent configuration. Its central outcome is a common physical picture: the carrier flow establishes particle organisation, while collisions redistribute that organisation in a regime- and scale-dependent way.

NUMERICAL METHODS

A verified computational foundation

Chronological hard-particle dynamics

I developed an event-driven solver for the force-free hard-disk problem and a forced kick–drift solver that couples continuous Stokes relaxation to chronologically resolved contacts within each ballistic drift. The force-free solver provides an independent reference within its stated assumptions.

Analytical single-particle comparisons, cross-solver checks, conservation diagnostics and timestep refinement establish the numerical scope used in the subsequent physical analyses. Reliability is assessed for the tested regimes and observables rather than claimed universally.

Matched simulations and multiscale diagnostics

The near-wall collision-on and collision-off calculations start from the same labelled particle state and experience the same prescribed carrier-flow record. I combine Lagrangian particle statistics with Eulerian density diagnostics to distinguish encounter availability, collision selection and the subsequent response to contact.

  • Collective structure: box-occupancy distributions, particle-number fluctuations, pair orientation and density autocorrelation.
  • Collision selection: near-contact pair availability, inward-motion exposure and environment-conditioned event yield.
  • Event and pair dynamics: radial rebound, separation, pair-axis memory, repeat-contact intervals and neighbourhood turnover.
  • Statistical assessment: matched comparisons, bootstrap uncertainty estimates and estimator-specific resolution checks.

SELECTED FINDINGS

Collisions act within a structure set by the flow

01 · The same collision law can reverse clustering

In alternating sinusoidal shear, exactly elastic collisions suppress the strong inhomogeneity of a high-inertia collisionless population. At low inertia, the same collision law produces pronounced banded inhomogeneity from an approximately homogeneous collisionless state. This reversal does not require collisional energy dissipation: the population response depends on competing particle, collision and carrier-transport timescales.

02 · Near-wall collisions redistribute organisation across scales

Both near-wall populations inherit a dominant streamwise orientation from the carrier streaks. Collisions do not create that preferred direction. Instead, they weaken short-range pair-axis alignment, reduce local particle-number fluctuations and increase streamwise density coherence within the existing anisotropic structure.

03 · Immediate rebound coexists with repeated local encounters

Elastic contact reverses inward normal relative motion, producing an immediate radial rebound. Nevertheless, approximately 87.9% of supported repeat-contact intervals show no resolved separation beyond two particle diameters before the next collision. Under equal-pair weighting, local recurrence increases from approximately 73% for pairs with two recorded collisions to approximately 92% for pairs with at least ten.

Selected pairs can collide repeatedly while the wider particle neighbourhood continues to turn over. Similarly, raw event counts alone do not measure collision propensity: conditioning on pair availability and inward-motion exposure separates the encounter population from configurations selected for contact.

MODEL & INTERPRETATION

What the study establishes

The detailed near-wall results concern a focused particle-response case, τp = 0.5, at one size, concentration and wall distance. A time-resolved carrier field is reconstructed from a three-dimensional turbulent-channel DNS at Reτ ≈ 360, while the particles themselves evolve as a two-dimensional wall-parallel hard-disk system.

Mechanically finite-sized, hydrodynamically unresolved. Particle diameter determines contact geometry and excluded volume. Carrier forcing uses centre-sampled Stokes relaxation with one-way coupling. Wall-normal transport, lubrication, lift, particle rotation, tangential contact forces and particle feedback to turbulence are outside the baseline model.

The matched design isolates the accumulated statistical consequence of enabling hard-particle interactions within this model. It does not provide an exact counterfactual for an individual collision after trajectories diverge. Isolated collision and collision–drag calculations are diagnostic references, and recurrence statistics remain subject to finite observation windows and saved-state cadence.

RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

From particle mechanics to reduced models

Ongoing extensions described in the thesis

The thesis outlines a staged extension of the confined particle model to rotation and frictional contact, together with a parallel continuum formulation using stochastic closure for unresolved collision history. The objective is to retain a tractable population description while representing the directional anisotropy, collision selection and recurrence measured at particle level.

Prospective control and optimisation

A longer-term direction is to introduce particle shape and flexibility as design variables, and use AI-assisted optimisation to target transport and preferential sampling in complex flows. Bayesian optimisation and reinforcement learning offer possible methods for this inverse-design problem. These are proposed research directions, distinct from the numerical and statistical investigations completed in the thesis.
