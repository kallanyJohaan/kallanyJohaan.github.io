Collective Dynamics of Colliding Inertial Particles in Prescribed Carrier Flows

What do local collisions do to the collective organisation that inertia and the surrounding flow create? The thesis answers in two settings: controlled alternating shear, and a reduced model of near-wall turbulence.

Johaan Tomy Kallany. Research internship with Team CALISTO, Inria Sophia Antipolis, April–September 2026, supervised by Christophe Henry and Laetitia Giraldi. Selected and funded through Inria’s 2026 Machine Learning + Simulation thematic semester.

From local contact to collective transport

Inertial particles do not follow fluid pathlines exactly: a finite response time can make the particle distribution non-uniform even in an incompressible carrier flow. Finite size adds a second ingredient. A contact impulse changes the relative velocity of two particles, and with it their later trajectories and encounters.

The thesis works through three connected stages: hard-particle numerical modelling, controlled alternating sinusoidal shear, and a reduced near-wall turbulent configuration. The common picture is that the carrier flow establishes particle organisation and collisions redistribute it, in a way that depends on regime and on scale.

My contribution

  • Developed and verified particle-dynamics solvers coupling continuous Stokes relaxation to discrete hard-particle contacts.
  • Reconstructed the time-resolved near-wall carrier field and designed matched simulations with collisions enabled and disabled.
  • Built particle, density and collision-conditioned diagnostics, with uncertainty and resolution checks, to connect local contact, pair recurrence and population-level redistribution.

The carrier-flow DNS was performed by Jérémie Bec (Institut de Physique de Nice) and supplied for this study. The reconstruction of that velocity record, the particle simulations and the statistical analysis are my own work. See in the thesis

Collisions act within a structure set by the flow

One collision law, two opposite outcomes

In alternating sinusoidal shear, exactly elastic collisions suppress the strong inhomogeneity of a high-inertia collisionless population. At low inertia, the same collision law produces pronounced banded inhomogeneity from an approximately homogeneous collisionless state. The reversal does not require collisional energy dissipation: the population response is set by competing particle, collision and carrier-transport timescales.

Near the wall, collisions reorganise a structure they did not create

Both near-wall populations inherit a dominant streamwise orientation from the carrier streaks; collisions do not create that preferred direction. Instead they weaken short-range pair-axis alignment, reduce local particle-number fluctuations and increase the streamwise coherence of the density field within the existing anisotropic structure.

Elastic rebound, then local return

Elastic contact reverses the inward normal relative motion and produces an immediate radial rebound. Even so, 87.9% of supported repeat-contact intervals show no resolved separation beyond two particle diameters before the next collision. Under equal-pair weighting, local recurrence rises from about 73% for pairs with two recorded collisions to about 92% for pairs with at least ten.

Selected pairs collide repeatedly while the wider neighbourhood keeps turning over. Likewise, raw event counts do not measure collision propensity: conditioning on pair availability and inward-motion exposure separates the encounter population from the configurations selected for contact.

A verified computational foundation

Chronological hard-particle dynamics

I developed an event-driven solver for the force-free hard-disk problem and a forced kick–drift solver that couples continuous Stokes relaxation to chronologically resolved contacts within each ballistic drift. The force-free solver is an independent reference within its stated assumptions.

Analytical single-particle comparisons, cross-solver checks, conservation diagnostics and timestep refinement fix the numerical scope used in the physical analyses. Reliability is assessed for the tested regimes and observables, not claimed in general.

Matched simulations and multiscale diagnostics

The collision-on and collision-off near-wall calculations start from the same labelled particle state and experience the same prescribed carrier-flow record. Lagrangian particle statistics are combined with Eulerian density diagnostics to separate encounter availability, collision selection and the response to contact.

  • Collective structure: box-occupancy distributions, particle-number fluctuations, pair orientation and density autocorrelation.
  • Collision selection: near-contact pair availability, inward-motion exposure and environment-conditioned event yield.
  • Event and pair dynamics: radial rebound, separation, pair-axis memory, repeat-contact intervals and neighbourhood turnover.
  • Statistical assessment: matched comparisons, bootstrap uncertainty estimates and estimator-specific resolution checks.

What the study establishes

The detailed near-wall results are for one prescribed relaxation time, τp = 0.5 in the DNS’s own nondimensional time units (τp+ ≈ 8 in viscous wall units), at one particle size, concentration and wall distance. A time-resolved carrier field is reconstructed from a three-dimensional turbulent-channel DNS at Reτ ≈ 360, while the particles evolve as a two-dimensional wall-parallel hard-disk system.

Mechanically finite-sized, hydrodynamically unresolved. Particle diameter sets contact geometry and excluded volume. Carrier forcing is centre-sampled Stokes relaxation with one-way coupling. Wall-normal transport, lubrication, lift, particle rotation, tangential contact forces and feedback on the turbulence are outside the baseline model.

The matched design isolates the accumulated statistical consequence of enabling hard-particle interactions within this model. It is not an exact counterfactual for an individual collision once trajectories diverge. Isolated collision and collision–drag calculations serve as diagnostic references, and the recurrence statistics remain subject to finite observation windows and saved-state cadence.

Limitations and extensions in the thesis

Future research: proposed

Continuum modelling and AI-based optimisation of flexible particle shapes

The complete research programme extends beyond the scope of one Master’s thesis. The present work develops the microscopic hard-particle framework, examines collision-modified collective behaviour in a controlled alternating sinusoidal shear flow, and applies the same particle model to a reconstructed wall-parallel carrier field from a turbulent channel-flow DNS. The two directions below extend that particle-level foundation.

Established in the thesis

A common computational framework for finite-size particles subject to continuous forcing and instantaneous hard-particle collisions, followed by controlled-flow and near-wall turbulent analyses of collision-modified collective organisation.

Ongoing and prospective directions

A reduced continuum description for wall-bounded turbulent carrier flows, and AI-based optimisation of flexible particle shapes. These directions are not results established by the completed particle-level study.

Long-term research direction: proposed

AI-based optimisation of flexible particle shapes

Research question. How can particle geometry and mechanical properties be selected to achieve prescribed preferential-sampling behaviour or to optimise transport?

A natural long-term research direction emerging from this thesis is to extend the present physics-based description from rigid finite-size particles to flexible particles whose geometry and mechanical properties influence their transport and preferential sampling of turbulent flows. Studies of non-spherical particles and deformable particle models show that particle geometry and flexibility can modify transport, orientation and preferential sampling in turbulence.

The present thesis provides a mechanistic foundation for this extension by examining how prescribed carrier flows, particle response, finite-size interactions, pair geometry and collision dynamics contribute to particle organisation. The associated inverse-design problem is to determine how particle geometry and mechanical properties can be selected to achieve prescribed preferential-sampling behaviour or to optimise transport.

Within the proposed reduced Stokes-flow framework, flexible particles are represented as assemblies of rigid spheres, with elastic deformation projected onto a finite-dimensional mechanical space. Such a formulation combines a reduced representation of compliant-body mechanics with bead-based low-Reynolds-number hydrodynamics. Particle geometry and mechanical properties then become design variables in an inverse-design problem.

Bayesian optimisation provides a natural approach for shape optimisation in such reduced hydrodynamic models, while reinforcement learning provides a complementary strategy for learning flow-dependent policies in complex carrier flows. These methods provide related methodological precedents rather than direct models of the flexible-particle problem considered here.

Proposed progression

  1. Represent flexible particles. Use assemblies of rigid spheres, with elastic deformation projected onto a finite-dimensional mechanical space.
  2. Define the inverse-design problem. Treat particle geometry and mechanical properties as design variables.
  3. Develop the methodology in controlled flows. Begin with controlled carrier flows, including cellular flows, before extending to turbulent environments of increasing complexity.
  4. Specify the preferential-sampling objective. Possible objectives include enhancing or suppressing preferential sampling of regions characterised by prescribed strain, vorticity or related flow quantities.

Optimisation methods

Bayesian optimisation provides a natural approach for shape optimisation in reduced hydrodynamic models. Reinforcement learning provides a complementary strategy for learning flow-dependent policies in complex carrier flows.

Scope of the thesis

The present thesis supplies the mechanistic particle-transport foundation. Particle shape, flexibility and AI-assisted optimisation remain prospective research directions.

This long-term research direction is set out in Section 5.1.4 of the thesis.

Parallel research direction: prescribed-flow formulation implemented; wall-bounded extension is the current objective

Continuum modelling for wall-bounded turbulent carrier flows

Research question. How should the existing stochastic collisional closure be generalised in an anisotropic turbulent environment, and must a one-particle density description be supplemented by separation- and orientation-resolved pair information?

In parallel with the particle-level work presented in this thesis, I am developing a continuum description of colliding inertial-particle populations derived from the underlying microscopic dynamics. The framework has already been implemented for analytically prescribed carrier flows, including the Solomon–Gollub cellular flow, and builds on the continuum approach of López and Puglisi.

Starting from the microscopic Stokes-drag and hard-particle collision model, the particle velocity is expanded in the particle response time. At higher order, the expansion contains unresolved collisional contributions involving derivatives of collision sums and terms associated with successive and repeated collision events.

Retaining the complete sequence of collision times, collision partners and contact orientations would preserve microscopic information that a reduced continuum description is intended to eliminate. The unresolved collisional remainder is therefore represented through a stochastic closure. Its singular collision-dependent contribution is modelled as an effective sequence of collision-localised random kicks whose covariance is related to the local collision-rate field. The underlying microscopic dynamics remain deterministic; the stochasticity enters only through the reduced description of unresolved collision history.

From microscopic dynamics to a reduced density equation

  1. Start from the microscopic model. Use the same Stokes-drag and hard-particle collision model developed for the particle-level work.
  2. Expand the particle velocity. An expansion in particle response time identifies higher-order unresolved collisional contributions involving derivatives of collision sums and successive or repeated collision events.
  3. Represent unresolved collision history. Model the singular collision-dependent remainder as collision-localised random kicks whose covariance is related to the local collision-rate field.
  4. Derive the density evolution. Use the closed particle-velocity representation to derive a reduced evolution equation for the particle-density field.

Statistical structure required in the wall-bounded extension

The near-wall analysis developed in Chapter 4 identifies additional statistical structure that the extension must represent:

  • Directional anisotropy.
  • Spatially non-uniform pair availability.
  • Orientation-dependent inward relative motion.
  • Preferential collision selection.
  • Substantial same-pair recurrence.
  • Wall-normal dependence of these statistics in an extension beyond the fixed wall-parallel plane.

Required state information

The particle-level measurements provide a basis for determining how the existing stochastic collisional closure should be generalised in an anisotropic turbulent environment and whether a one-particle density description must be supplemented by separation- and orientation-resolved pair information.

Aim of the extension

The aim is to construct and assess a reduced continuum model that retains the tractability of the stochastic closure while incorporating the collision statistics and anisotropic organisation identified in the present near-wall turbulent setting.

This parallel research direction is described in Section 5.1.3 of the thesis. Foundational reference: C. López and A. Puglisi, Continuum description of finite-size particles advected by external flows: The effect of collisions, Physical Review E 69, 046306 (2004). Read the paper ↗.

Common foundation. The present thesis supplies the mechanistic particle-transport foundation. The continuum direction extends the reduced description to the wall-bounded setting, while the flexible-particle direction introduces design variables and AI-assisted optimisation.
