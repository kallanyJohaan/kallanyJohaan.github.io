RESEARCH

Understanding particle dynamics across scales.

My work sits at the intersection of computational fluid mechanics, numerical particle dynamics and statistical analysis. I study how local inertia and interparticle interactions shape large-scale organisation in prescribed carrier flows.

Carrier-flow structure organises the population; collisions redistribute it in a regime- and scale-dependent way.

COMPUTATIONAL APPROACH

From numerical mechanics to physical interpretation

The modelling choices are intentionally explicit: finite-size contact geometry, a prescribed one-way-coupled carrier field, and careful numerical/statistical checks.

01 / PARTICLE MODEL

Hybrid continuous–discrete dynamics

Stokes-drag evolution is paired with instantaneous hard-particle impulses; an event-driven force-free reference supports numerical verification.

02 / FLOW RECONSTRUCTION

Controlled and DNS-derived fields

Alternating sinusoidal shear provides a tractable comparison, while a reconstructed turbulent-channel wall-parallel field introduces near-wall structure.

03 / COMPARATIVE DESIGN

Matched collision-on / collision-off runs

Paired simulations begin from matching particle states and experience the same carrier record to clarify the statistical effect of enabling contacts.

04 / VALIDATION

Verification and uncertainty

Conservation tests, timestep refinement, flow-field interpolation checks, statistical confidence bands and sensitivity analysis support interpretation.

NEAR-WALL TURBULENCE

Collisions can reorganise structure across scales.

In the focused reduced near-wall case, contacts modify particle-number fluctuations, pair-axis alignment and streamwise density coherence without eliminating the organisation imposed by turbulent streaks.

COLLISION RECURRENCE

Repeated contact is not the same as collective trapping.

Collision-conditioned analysis identifies local same-pair recurrences, while neighbourhood-retention measurements distinguish these encounters from long-lived many-particle confinement.
