Research directions

From particle-level dynamics to reduced continuum modelling and prospective inverse design.

My MSc thesis develops a microscopic hard-particle framework, examines collision-modified collective behaviour in a controlled alternating sinusoidal shear flow, and applies the same particle model to a reconstructed wall-parallel carrier field from a turbulent channel-flow DNS. An ongoing parallel direction aims to extend an existing stochastic continuum formulation to wall-bounded turbulent carrier flows, while a long-term prospective direction concerns AI-based optimisation of flexible particle shapes.

01 Completed: MSc thesis

Particle dynamics and collective transport

I developed a timestep-free, event-scheduled hard-disk solver and a forced kick–drift solver that combines continuous Stokes relaxation with chronologically resolved hard-particle contacts. Matched collision-on and collision-off calculations show that the carrier field establishes the dominant particle organisation and that collisions redistribute this organisation across scales.

How do finite-size interparticle collisions modify the collective organisation generated by inertial transport, from controlled carrier-flow regimes to near-wall turbulence?

Master’s thesis, 2026

Collective Dynamics of Colliding Inertial Particles in Prescribed Carrier Flows

My MSc thesis investigates how finite-size interparticle collisions modify collective organisation generated by inertial transport in prescribed carrier flows. Using numerically assessed particle-dynamics solvers and matched collision-on and collision-off simulations, I examine spatial inhomogeneity, density correlations and collision-conditioned dynamics in an alternating sinusoidal shear flow and a reduced near-wall turbulent configuration.

The research was carried out with Team CALISTO at Inria Sophia Antipolis under the supervision of Dr. Christophe Henry and Dr. Laetitia Giraldi during an internship selected and funded through Inria’s ML+SIM 2026 thematic semester.

Principal findings

  • Regime-dependent response under the same elastic collision law

    In the tested high-inertia regime, exactly elastic collisions suppress the strong spatial inhomogeneity of the collisionless population; in the tested low-inertia regime, the same collision law generates substantial banded inhomogeneity from an approximately homogeneous collisionless state. Because the two regimes also use different carrier parameters, this is a regime-level reversal rather than an isolated particle-inertia effect. The reversal does not require collisional energy dissipation; the outcome depends on the combined dynamical regime established by particle response, collision frequency, restitution and carrier transport.

    Shown: the low-inertia regime, collisions off and on. See in the thesis · Download figure

  • Scale-dependent redistribution within a carrier-imposed structure

    The carrier streaks impose the dominant streamwise orientation of both populations. Collisions do not create this preferred direction; they weaken short-range pair-axis alignment, reduce particle-number fluctuations and increase the streamwise coherence of the particle-density field.

    Shown: density autocorrelation, collisions on. See in the thesis · Download figure

  • Immediate radial rebound, then local recurrence

    Among completed repeat intervals with at least one interior saved state, 87.9% show no resolved separation beyond two particle diameters before the next impact. Because the saved-state cadence is finite, this indicates resolved local recurrence rather than uninterrupted confinement. Under equal-pair weighting, the fraction rises from about 73% for pairs with two recorded collisions to about 92% for pairs with at least ten.

    Shown: local-recurrence fraction against pair multiplicity. See in the thesis · Download figure

Computational and statistical approach

  • An event-driven hard-disk solver (force-free reference) and a kick–drift solver coupling Stokes relaxation to chronologically resolved contacts.
  • Verification against analytical single-particle solutions, cross-solver comparisons, conservation diagnostics and timestep-refinement studies.
  • Matched collision-on and collision-off runs from the same particle state in the same carrier-flow record.
  • Box-occupancy, pair-orientation, density-autocorrelation and collision-conditioned statistics with bootstrap uncertainty and resolution checks.

The detailed near-wall results concern the focused τp = 0.5 case (τp+ ≈ 8) within a reduced, one-way-coupled wall-parallel hard-disk model, for one particle size, concentration, wall-normal location and turbulent carrier record.
