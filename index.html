What I work on

Which particle-scale mechanisms matter for collective transport, and what must a tractable model keep?

  • Flow and inertia

    How do flow structure and particle response time shape trajectories, preferential concentration and dispersion?

  • Collisions and collective structure

    When do local contacts reorganise clustering, pair statistics and spatial coherence?

  • Reduced models

    What must a reduced or stochastic description retain to reproduce what the particles actually do?

Master’s thesis, 2026

Collective Dynamics of Colliding Inertial Particles in Prescribed Carrier Flows

Six months with Team CALISTO at Inria Sophia Antipolis, supervised by Christophe Henry and Laetitia Giraldi, in an internship selected and funded through Inria’s 2026 Machine Learning + Simulation thematic semester.

One question in two settings: what do hard-particle collisions do to the organisation that inertia and the carrier flow create? First in controlled alternating shear, then in a reduced model of near-wall turbulence driven by a channel-flow DNS at Reτ ≈ 360.

I wrote and verified the particle solvers, reconstructed the time-resolved carrier field from the DNS, and ran matched simulations with collisions on and off, with the particle, density and collision-conditioned diagnostics needed to compare them.

What the thesis found

  • One collision law, two opposite outcomes

    In alternating shear, elastic collisions erase the clustering of a high-inertia population and create banded clustering where a low-inertia population was nearly uniform. No collisional dissipation is needed; the outcome is set by competing particle, collision and transport timescales.

    Thesis Fig. 3.3, p. 36

  • Near the wall, collisions reorganise a structure they did not create

    Carrier streaks set the streamwise orientation of both populations. Collisions weaken short-range pair alignment, damp local number fluctuations and lengthen the streamwise coherence of the density field.

    Thesis Fig. 4.8, p. 66

  • Elastic rebound, then local return

    After contact, 87.9% of repeat-contact intervals never resolve a separation beyond two particle diameters. The share rises from about 73% for pairs that collide twice to about 92% for pairs that collide ten times or more.

    Thesis Fig. 4.15, p. 81

The near-wall results hold for a two-dimensional, one-way-coupled hard-disk model on a wall-parallel plane, at one inertia (τp+ ≈ 8), one particle size and one concentration.
