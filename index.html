RESEARCH APPROACH

Connecting particle mechanics
with transport at larger scales

My aim is to understand which particle-scale mechanisms matter for collective transport, and how to represent them in computationally tractable models.

Flow structures organise particle motion; inertia changes the response to those structures; interactions modify subsequent trajectories and encounters. I investigate these connections through controlled simulations and statistical diagnostics. A longer-term interest is to use the resulting models to guide the design and control of particle transport.

A CONCRETE STUDY · MSc THESIS

Collisions in prescribed carrier flows

INRIA CALISTO · APRIL–SEPTEMBER 2026

Collective Dynamics of Colliding Inertial Particles in Prescribed Carrier Flows

My six-month research internship examined how inertia and hard-particle collisions alter organisation in controlled shear flows and a reduced near-wall turbulent configuration. The work was supervised by Dr Christophe Henry and Dr Laetitia Giraldi.

My contribution

I developed and validated particle-dynamics solvers, reconstructed the prescribed carrier field from existing DNS data, and implemented matched particle and density diagnostics for simulations with collisions enabled and disabled.

  • Alternating shear: elastic collisions increased inhomogeneity at low inertia and reduced it at high inertia.
  • Reduced near-wall model: collisions weakened short-range pair alignment while increasing streamwise density coherence.

The near-wall conclusions concern a two-dimensional, one-way-coupled particle model driven by a prescribed turbulent carrier field.
