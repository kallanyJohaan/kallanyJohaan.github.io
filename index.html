MATHEMATICAL MODELLING · PARTICLE-LADEN FLOWS

From particle interactions to collective transport.

I investigate how fluid motion, particle inertia and collisions shape the distribution and transport of dispersed particles. My work combines particle-level simulations with statistical analysis to connect microscale interactions with clustering, spatial correlations and collective behaviour in complex flows.

THE RESEARCH QUESTION

What changes when particles collide?

A carrier flow already structures where inertial particles travel, accumulate and encounter one another. I study what happens when hard-particle contacts are introduced—and how local interactions affect the wider population.

The focus is not a single trajectory, but the connection between mechanics, transport and statistical organisation.

FEATURED WORK

An event-driven view of collective dynamics.

My MSc thesis develops a computational framework for finite-size inertial-particle collisions, then examines their influence in prescribed analytic flows and a reduced near-wall turbulent setting.

Matched collision-on and collision-off simulations help isolate how contacts reorganise a particle population.
